## Step 0 — Why diversify

Diversifying means **not depending on a single bet**. The goal isn’t to maximize returns, but to reduce the damage when one asset performs poorly.

Note before you start:

- **horizon** (1 year, 5 years, 10 years)
- **risk tolerance** (what do you do at −30%?)
- **ability to leave it alone** — not touching the invested money

A sustainable diversification is one you understand and keep during stress.

## Step 1 — Asset classes

The first lever of diversification is spreading across asset families that behave differently:

- **stocks** (long-term growth, high volatility)
- **bonds** (income, often more stable depending on duration)
- **cash** / equivalents (liquidity, buffer, opportunities)
- **alternative assets** (listed real estate, commodities, possibly crypto — high volatility)

Each class has its role: growth, stability, liquidity. The mix depends on your profile, not on a magic formula.

## Step 2 — Geography and sectors

Within equities, avoid concentrating everything in one country or one sector:

- **geographies** (domestic market, international, emerging)
- **sectors** (technology, healthcare, energy, finance…)
- **company sizes** (large, mid, small caps)

A 100% technology portfolio from a single country is not diversified, even with many positions.

## Step 3 — Correlation

Real diversification comes from **low correlation**: assets that don’t all rise and fall at the same time.

- two global equity ETFs are **highly correlated** → little added diversification
- stocks and bonds often react differently → useful diversification
- caution: correlations can **increase in a crisis**, exactly when you need them most

Simple rule: ask yourself *“if this one falls, does the other necessarily fall too?”*

## Step 4 — Define an allocation

Set a **target allocation** in percentages, aligned with your profile:

- cautious profile → more bonds and cash
- dynamic profile → more stocks
- “satellite” bucket (e.g. crypto) → small and owned

Write the target allocation **before** you invest. It’s your compass when the market moves and emotion pushes you to improvise.

## Step 5 — Rebalancing

Over time, a winning bucket grows and distorts the allocation. Rebalancing brings the portfolio back toward the target:

- **by calendar** (e.g. once a year)
- **by thresholds** (e.g. if a bucket exceeds the target by X points)

Rebalancing too often costs fees and taxes; never letting a bucket take all the risk is worse. Find a sustainable rhythm.

## Step 6 — Limits of diversification

Diversification is not absolute protection:

- it **does not remove** overall market risk
- **too many positions** makes tracking hard without really reducing risk
- **complexity is a risk**: an unreadable portfolio ends up poorly managed

Diversifying isn’t stacking products, it’s spreading intelligently.

## A simple allocation example

As an illustration (not advice), a balanced portfolio might look like:

- **global equities** (via a broad ETF) → core of the portfolio
- **bonds** → relative stability
- **cash** → liquidity and opportunities
- **satellite bucket** (optional, small) → a more volatile asset, owned

The exact percentages depend on your horizon and risk tolerance.

## Checklist

- [ ] Horizon and risk tolerance written down
- [ ] Spread across several asset classes
- [ ] Geographic and sector diversification verified
- [ ] Correlation taken into account (no false diversification)
- [ ] Target allocation defined in percentages
- [ ] Rebalancing rule chosen
- [ ] Portfolio readable and manageable

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Confusing number of positions with diversification**: 15 stocks in the same sector don’t diversify.
- **Putting everything on the latest trend** (one sector, one crypto) without a clear allocation.
- **Never rebalancing**, then ending up with a single bucket dominating the risk.
- **Stacking products** you don’t understand, in the name of diversification.
- **Forgetting overall market risk** and believing you’re shielded from any decline.
