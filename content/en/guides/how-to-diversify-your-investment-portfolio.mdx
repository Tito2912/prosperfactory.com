## Step 0 — Clarify your goal

Diversification only makes sense once you know what you’re protecting. Write your goal, then add 2–3 constraints:

- **Grow long term:** the priority is broad coverage and low costs over years.
- **Preserve capital:** the priority is limiting large drawdowns.
- **Take some risk for upside:** you accept volatility in a small part of the portfolio.

Helpful constraints to note:

- time horizon (1 year, 5 years, 10 years)
- how much a temporary 30–50% drop would affect your decisions
- deposit currency (EUR, USD…) and FX exposure
- how much time you can spend maintaining the portfolio

## Step 1 — Spread across asset classes

The first layer of diversification is across asset classes, because they respond differently to the same events:

- **Equities:** growth potential, higher volatility.
- **Bonds / fixed income:** typically steadier, sensitive to interest rates.
- **Cash / equivalents:** stability and flexibility, but eroded by inflation.
- **Real assets (real estate, commodities):** different drivers, can hedge some scenarios.
- **Crypto:** high volatility, treated as a small satellite for most people.

The point isn’t to own everything. It’s to avoid betting your entire plan on one type of asset.

## Step 2 — Diversify within each asset class

Once you’ve chosen asset classes, spread within them too:

- **Equities:** many companies, not one or two. Broad funds do this automatically.
- **Bonds:** mix of issuers and maturities rather than a single line.
- **Crypto:** avoid putting everything in one obscure token.

A single fund holding hundreds of companies is often more diversified than a hand-picked basket of five stocks you happen to like.

## Step 3 — Add geographic and sector spread

Concentration hides in familiar places:

- **Geography:** a home-country bias is common. Adding other regions reduces the impact of one economy underperforming.
- **Sectors:** if most of your holdings are in the same industry (tech, energy, banks), you’re exposed to that sector’s cycle.
- **Currency:** foreign holdings add FX exposure—neither good nor bad, but worth knowing.

Check the underlying holdings of your funds; two “different” products can overlap heavily.

## Step 4 — Mind correlation

Diversification is about how holdings move relative to each other, not how many you own.

- If everything rises and falls together, you have concentration wearing a diversification costume.
- Assets that behave differently in the same environment provide real balance.
- During crises, correlations can spike—things that usually diverge sometimes fall together.

A quick self-check: if one theme collapsed, how much of your portfolio would move? If the answer is “most of it,” you’re less diversified than the number of positions suggests.

## Step 5 — Size your crypto sleeve

Crypto can add diversification, but its volatility means position size matters more than usual:

- Decide a maximum percentage before buying, not after a rally.
- Keep it small enough that a large drawdown wouldn’t force a panicked exit.
- Consider spreading entries over time (recurring buys) instead of a single lump sum.

Treat it as a satellite around a diversified core, not the core itself.

## Step 6 — Rebalance on a schedule

Over time, winners grow and quietly concentrate your portfolio. Rebalancing brings it back to your plan:

- **Calendar-based:** review once or twice a year.
- **Threshold-based:** rebalance when an allocation drifts past a set band (for example, ±5%).
- Keep costs and taxes in mind—don’t trade constantly just to chase precision.

Write the rule down. A plan you follow beats a perfect plan you abandon.

## Sample allocations

These are illustrative structures, not recommendations. Adapt to your goal and risk tolerance:

- **Conservative:** large fixed-income and cash portion, smaller equity portion, no or tiny crypto.
- **Balanced:** meaningful equity core, some fixed income, small real-asset and crypto satellites.
- **Growth:** equity-heavy core across regions, modest fixed income, small crypto satellite.

The exact numbers matter less than the principle: a diversified core, small satellites, and a rebalancing rule.

## Checklist

- I defined my goal and horizon before choosing allocations.
- I spread across several asset classes, not just one.
- I diversified within each class (issuers, companies, tokens).
- I checked geographic and sector concentration.
- I looked for overlap between my funds.
- I sized volatile sleeves (like crypto) intentionally.
- I set a rebalancing rule I can actually follow.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Confusing quantity with diversification:** many similar holdings still move together.
- **Ignoring overlap:** owning several funds that hold the same top companies.
- **Home bias:** concentrating everything in one country or currency.
- **Oversizing a hot asset:** letting one winner dominate the portfolio.
- **Never rebalancing:** letting drift turn a plan into a bet.
- **Over-diversifying:** adding products for the sake of it, raising fees and complexity without reducing risk.
